Two research domains, one method

I develop predictive and decision-support methods for infrastructure and built assets. Most questions start with a problem a government department or the industry has brought to me.

Infrastructure performance, risk and delivery

Cost and schedule performance, contingency, workforce capacity, safety, rework and megaproject benchmarking. My recent work shows that reference class forecasting and probabilistic risk analysis are complementary rather than competing, and sets out how to use them together in public-works estimates.

Shared methods

Machine learning and deep learning, including LSTM forecasting; probabilistic risk modelling and simulation; system dynamics; complex-network analysis; and multi-criteria decision models. Next, I’m moving towards interpretable AI and decision tools that agencies can run themselves.

A surveyor first, then an academic

I spent my first eight years in the Hong Kong Government, in the surveying discipline of the Buildings Department and the Architectural Services Department, working on condition surveys, defect diagnosis, statutory control and enforcement. That work taught me that good decisions balance technical limits, regulation, budgets and people. It still shapes what I research and how I teach.

Since 2012 I’ve worked full time in universities in Australia and Hong Kong. I took my PhD at Curtin University in Perth, stayed on as Senior Lecturer, and later spent four years as Associate Professor at the University of Newcastle, where I led the Master of Project Management through accreditation by the Australian Institute of Project Management. I’m now at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, and I keep close ties with Australia as an Adjunct Professor at the University of Canberra and an assessor for the Australian Research Council.
